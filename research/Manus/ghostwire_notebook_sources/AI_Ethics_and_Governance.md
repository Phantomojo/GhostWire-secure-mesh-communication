## AI: Ethics and Governance in GhostWire's Decentralized Network

The integration of Artificial Intelligence into GhostWire's secure and decentralized mesh network brings immense power but also significant ethical considerations. Ensuring responsible AI development and deployment, particularly in sensitive contexts like censorship resistance and crisis communication, is paramount. This document outlines GhostWire's approach to AI ethics and governance.

### 1. Core Ethical Principles for GhostWire AI

GhostWire's AI development is guided by principles that prioritize user well-being, privacy, and the integrity of the decentralized network:

*   **Transparency**: Users should understand how AI influences network behavior, routing decisions, and threat detection. While the underlying models can be complex, their general function and impact should be explainable.
*   **Fairness and Bias Mitigation**: AI models must be developed and trained to avoid perpetuating or amplifying existing societal biases. This is especially critical in threat detection, where biased models could unfairly target certain users or traffic patterns.
*   **Privacy by Design**: AI features are designed to operate with minimal access to sensitive user data. Techniques like Federated Learning ensure that raw data remains on the device, and only aggregated, anonymized insights are shared.
*   **Accountability**: Mechanisms should be in place to identify and address issues arising from AI decisions, allowing for human oversight and intervention when necessary.
*   **Robustness and Reliability**: AI systems must be resilient to adversarial attacks and operate reliably even in challenging network conditions, as their failure could have severe consequences for communication.
*   **Human Oversight and Control**: AI should augment, not replace, human decision-making. Users and network administrators should retain ultimate control over critical functions.

### 2. Addressing Bias in AI Models

**Challenge**: AI models can inadvertently learn and amplify biases present in their training data. In a mesh network, this could lead to discriminatory threat detection or routing decisions.

**Mitigation Strategies**:

*   **Diverse and Representative Data**: While raw user data is not centralized, efforts are made to ensure that synthetic or anonymized datasets used for initial model training are diverse and representative of various network conditions and user behaviors.
*   **Fairness Metrics**: Implementing fairness metrics during model evaluation to detect and correct for disparate impact across different user groups or network segments.
*   **Adversarial Training**: Training models to be robust against attempts to introduce bias or manipulate their behavior.
*   **Regular Audits**: Periodically auditing AI models for unintended biases and performance disparities.
